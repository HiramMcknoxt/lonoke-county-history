Where the record lives

Lonoke / Prairie Regional Library System — genealogy program

The single richest confirmed holding: the system's historical archives focus on Lonoke and Prairie Counties and include microfilm of the Lonoke Democrat, the Carlisle Independent, and the England Democrat, plus Lonoke County cemetery records. Resident genealogist Leisa Horness is based at the Arlene Cherry Memorial Library in Cabot (Mon–Tue 8–5, Wed 8–12) · 1 (855) 572-6657 ext. 102.

The England Democrat office, England

A bound copy of every back issue since 1934, kept at 121 E. Haywood St., where a historic newspaper museum is being built into the renovated office. The paper's front-page Yesteryear column is drawn from this archive.

Lonoke County Library System

Holds the Cabot Star-Herald microfilm (in-person, Cabot branch). A digitization inquiry covering the Star-Herald run was sent November 4, 2026 and now serves both repositories.

Arkansas State Archives — Arkansas Digital Newspaper Project

The Archives hold roughly 3,000 Arkansas newspaper titles and run the state's National Digital Newspaper Program work: 300,000+ pages digitized from 25+ counties, free on Chronicling America — including part of the Carlisle Independent.

Chronicling America (Library of Congress)

chroniclingamerica.loc.gov — free searchable page images, and the U.S. Newspaper Directory behind the inventory on this site.

University of Arkansas Libraries

A sortable spreadsheet of Arkansas newspapers on microfilm (by title, county, and city) — the cross-check for what exists on film statewide.

Photographs — where the pictures come from

  • Encyclopedia of Arkansas — the county's photograph backbone: every photograph tagged for Lonoke County in the Encyclopedia's media library was reviewed, and the collection on the Photographs page carries each picture with its caption and credit line exactly as published, linked back to its Encyclopedia page. The photographs remain the property of their credited holders and are gathered here for local-history reference by a non-commercial community project; any credited holder can have a photograph removed on request.
  • Library of Congress — Carol M. Highsmith's 2020 Scott and Plum Bayou photographs, Dorothea Lange's 1938 Farm Security Administration photographs near England, and the American Red Cross drought-relief photographs of 1930–31 — public domain or published with no known restrictions, as each caption states.
  • Lonoke County government — the courthouse photograph (Brandonrush, CC BY-SA 4.0) and a c.1918 Signal Corps aerial of Eberts Field (public domain), via the county's own site.
  • Not yet in hand — some forty further photographs identified on Wikimedia Commons (Carlisle's banks and depot, Keo's commercial district, Lonoke's historic houses, Camp Nelson, Marlsgate itself) whose per-file licenses could not be verified in this pass after Commons rate-limited the check; they will be added only when each license is read on its own file page — licenses are never guessed. The Joe Hogan Fish Hatchery's own photographs are Arkansas Game and Fish Commission material and await the Commission's permission.

Method, in one paragraph

Every document and pointer here comes from a named source. Government records are public records. Copyrighted news coverage is never reproduced: the site carries a title, date, short summary and a link, and nothing more. Contributed material is reviewed before it is posted and credited as the contributor specifies; oral histories are transcribed and the transcript is what is indexed.

The Lonoke County Lore Repository is a public, non-commercial history project. Its sister site, The Cabot Lore Repository, covers the city of Cabot in depth. In-copyright newspapers are indexed as links, citations, and summaries — full text appears only for public-domain or permitted material. Photographs are shown with their credits; any credited holder can have a photograph removed on request.
